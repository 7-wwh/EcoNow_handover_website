Item 01 — Origin

Where it started

EcoNow started with the sole idea of benefacting children that are forced to leave the Homes that they've long stayed due to rising issues of not enough space and resources to keep supporting them after their legal age. Once these children hit their legal age limit, the institutional safety net vanishes overnight. Hence, EcoNow planned to tackle this issue by providing real-life hands-on entrepreneurial skills for them, hoping to better equip them for the unforeseeable future.

The Foster Care Cliff (Social Bottleneck)

  • • Shelter homes face intense structural bottlenecks and severe space constraints.
  • • Homes are forced to make room for younger cohorts, pushing out newly-minted legal adults.
  • • Transitioning youth are left with no financial runway, no family fallbacks, and very little real-world preparation.

Environmental Hazard (Used Cooking Oil)

  • • Local restaurants and residential communities constantly dump toxic used cooking oil down sewage systems.
  • • Hardened grease creates severe blockages, pipe damage, and ecosystem disruption.
  • • What was once treated as hazardous environmental sludge can be cleanly salvaged as a free fuel feedstock.

Item 03 — Current State

Where the compass drifted

The current state of EcoNow has lost its original focus (according to Dr Roy) which we're focus on profiting instead of trying to educate children and equip them with skills. Which I totally agree but EcoNow's mission has faced some constraints / ceilings that will be further discussed in the next section.

EcoNow's goal misorientation can be dated back to the participation of UCSE competition, which is a competition that is held by the MOHE (Ministry of Higher Education) which grant us RM 40,000.

According to Dr Roy, the organizer (MOHE) has deviated the original idea of the competition from supporting university students with Social Entrepreneurship to pushing students to register and start our independent Social Enterprise by registering with SSM and related government parties.

Dr. Roy's Position: As of the current state of the competition, Dr Roy strongly disagree with the idea of registering and starting social enterprise due to we as a student run club doesn't have the time and the capabilities to run a consistent profiting enterprise.

Item 04 — The Ceiling

Before you become the OM (Operational Manager)

Before you become the Operational Manager (OM) you need to realize that EcoNow has hit a ceiling where restructuring is needed. Here's what I mean.

Why the Ceiling is Reached (Operational Reality)

Our calculations prove that even under absolute maximum, best-case production capacities, EcoNow is mathematically bounded by fixed structural limits. Enactus only runs for 12 weeks per semester (approx. 3 operational months). In that narrow window, a single monthly teaching session with our beneficiaries limits our maximum output.

With high commercial booth rentals, transportation, R&D costs, and now operational labor wages, trying to run a formal SSM enterprise with this model fails. To earn more money, you cannot simply "sell more candles" because doing so forces repetitive production, which completely destroys the educational value for the beneficiaries and burns out a student run volunteer club.

Item 05 — Constraints

Why the ceiling holds

Constraint 01 — Physical Limits

Production Capacity of Beneficiaries

"Remember, you are working with children."
  • • Repetitive work kills instruction: Can our beneficiaries produce every single month? Things will get repetitive fast. Repetition is exactly where the educational value quietly disappears.
  • • Chaotic coordination: Sometimes candles produced with beneficiaries are chaotic and require heavy student supervision. The produced candles might not be up to standard for commercial retail.
  • • Complex formulas are unfair: Beneficiaries are not able to handle complex candle formulas, as some of them might be already struggling with basic mathematics.
  • • Heavy logistical overhead: Every production session with a beneficiary takes a significant amount of time (setup, teaching, coordination, transport, and cleanup). Doing this once a month requires immense member dedication.
Constraint 02 — Strategy

Costing & Product Quality

The Bread Shop Metaphor: Today you're planning to open a bread shop that benefits the underprivileged community by teaching them real-life skills of bread-making while operating as a business.

Option A: Open Fast & Shallow

You set up the shop, learn basic bread-making techniques, and immediately start selling and teaching. Resulting in "meh bread, meh flow of customers, and meh money".

Product Quality 30%
Customer Retention 20%
"To obtain more profit, we build quality."
Alternative — Volume Tactic

The Volume Alternative

Not going to lie to you, there's another method that's able to grant EcoNow more money: Selling cheap.

Have you ever thought of how cheap RM5 chicken rice is still able to sustain in this kind of an economy? Volume is the secret.

We can sell candles, lots of candles, at a very cheap price—say RM20. We go to a charity carnival and hope to sell a ton of candles; we might be able to make lots of money as well.

The Operational Stress-Test:
  • We are hoping that we sell lots of candles. Will the customers buy again?
  • Are we able to produce a cheap but high-quality product?
"It's your turn to answer this question."
